# Construction

> *A gate never built is a war never won cheaply.*
> -- Ministry of War memorandum, year 4019

Shipyards make ships on their own, every year, from raw materials --
that is production, covered in chapter 3. **Construction** here means
the eight big installations that take a site, years, and a steady
delivery line of cargo:

| Structure | Years | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SRM field | 2 | Mines the sector; damages fleets that jump through it |
| Command base | 6 | A mobile starbase: scouting, scanning, fleet support, jump base |
| Fortress | 12 | The immovable one: the strongest fixed defense in the game |
| Industrial complex | 10 | Links the surrounding mine worlds and concentrates their output |
| Outpost | 3 | A cheap sensor and a flag planted in someone else's back yard |
| Stargate | 15 | The jump corridor itself -- the reason level-10 tech ends wars |
| Warp link | 5 | A one-way door between exactly two friendly bases |
| Jumpspace disrupter | 8 | Denies enemy jump movement around itself |

A site consumes its materials **per year while it stands** (the table
is in the appendix), and an unfinished site is a target: enemies can
destroy one as easily as a world, and *Build ▸ Abort construction*
tears yours down if the money stops making sense. The universe holds
fifty sites at a time; when *New construction site* says there is no
room, somebody else's project is using it.

## Bases

Command bases and fortresses carry destinations like fleets and
crawl one sector a year, burning trillum whether blocked or not; all
other base types are fixed once built. Bases of any kind:

- scout what is around them and scan for what is not, which is the
  only way to learn about objects your fleets never visit;
- act as the jump base for fleets sitting on them;
- refuel fleets;
- cannot enter an occupied sector at all -- and a base being shoved
  sideways by its own pathing can actually be pushed off the playable
  grid, because the original checked its detours nowhere. Watch bases
  that hug the edges.

An **industrial complex** is a different animal: it is the grid
connector between the eight neighbouring sectors. Each year it pulls
raw material from friendly mine worlds that hold a working reserve,
and pushes its own overflow back out to the same mines when its
hold is full. Clustering chemical plants, metal mines and trillum
mines around a complex is how late-game empires pay for gate
construction without bankrupting a sector.

## Gates, links and disrupters

A **stargate** sends jump-capable fleets -- jump fleets and
hunter-killer forces, not standard warp traffic -- between any two
gates tuned to the same **frequency**, a number 0 to 9999. Frequencies
are the diplomacy of movement: *Empire ▸ Link frequencies* retunes a
gate, so a captured enemy gate can be folded into your network -- or
a retune can strand every fleet that was counting on the old pairing.

A **warp link** is a gate built as a pair: it connects your base to
exactly one far end, permanently, and needs none of the frequency
diplomacy a stargate does. Five years instead of fifteen; a corridor
you can widen only by building more of them.

A **jumpspace disrupter** makes the space around it hostile to jump
drives: enemy jump fleets cannot make jump moves through its field,
while *your own* warp-driven fleets riding inside a friendly
disrupter's field move at jump speed -- ten sectors a year on
hulls that normally crawl one. A disrupter ringed around a choke point
is the strongest defensive structure in the game and the most
expensive thing to fight through.

## Fortresses, gates and the two rules that surprise people

A **fortress** multiplies the value of everything a defended sector
is worth -- the combat table's base adjustment makes a fortress
sector worth two and a half times an open one -- and at twelve
years' construction, it is a statement about where you intend to
stand and not move. It also does something no manual written before
1990 mentioned politely: **a fleet that departs from under a
fortress is catapulted four sectors along its way** before it even
starts. Fortresses are not defense. They are short-range logistics
with a gun on top.

**A fleet entering a stargate arrives the next year, whatever the
distance.** One sector away or across the map -- gates move jump
traffic at a flat one year per jump, and only the two ends need
their frequencies in agreement. Ten-sector jumps from jump bases
pale next to this, which is why gate construction (fifteen years)
decides wars decades before anyone fires in the sector they connect.

**SRM fields** (space minefields) damage anything that *jumps*
through the sector -- hunter-killers, jumpships, jumptransports --
and do nothing to warp traffic, which makes them chokepoint tools
along gate lanes rather than area denial in general. They are also
mortal: *Fleet ▸ SRM sweep* clears a field the slow way, one
careful pass at a time, and an unescorted sweeper in enemy space is
a letter the enemy will never forward.
