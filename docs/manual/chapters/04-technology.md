# Technology and Research

> *No one steals a civilization's tools from an armory that has
> nothing left to lose.*
> -- Guardian proverb, date of composition disputed

## Eleven levels

Technology runs from **0, pre-tech**, through primitive,
pre-atomic, atomic, pre-warp, warp, jump, bio-tech, starship and
pre-gate, to **10, gate** -- the drive technology that scattered
the old empire and the ceiling of what anyone here can reach again.

Each level unlocks its kit: atomic tech opens trillum mining and
ground-defense missiles, pre-warp gives you fighters and command
bases, warp gives transports, jump gives the jumpships and the
bases that carry them, bio-tech opens hunter-killers, penetrators,
ambrosia and outposts, starship tech brings the capital ships, LAMs
and ninjas, and gate tech -- fifteen years a site -- brings stargate
links and disrupters that rewrite the geometry of the whole map. The
appendix's technology table lists exactly what each level first
makes possible.

Every **world** carries its own technology level as well, and the
two advance together only through the empire's research effort.

## How research actually happens

Once per year, each active empire rolls for discovery. The chance
is built from the empire's **labs**, of which there are four kinds:

| Lab | Annual chance |
| --- | --- |
| Capital world | 12% |
| University world (at the empire's level) | 15% |
| Any world that has out-researched the empire | 15% |
| Ruins world | 5% |
| University standing on ruins | 17% |

Starbases count too, with a shorter list and no ruins bonus. Only
the twenty best labs count at all.

The roll is **breadth before depth**. An empire that is missing any
single technology of its current tier is spending its research on
that hole -- you cannot skip a level by getting lucky -- and only an
empire whose tier is complete can advance to the next one at all.
The last technology of a tier is the lock on the door. When the
empire does advance, its capital and every university and lab world
come up one level with it, which is how a research empire snowballs
instead of leaving its home worlds behind.

The third row of the table is the interesting one: **conquest is
research**. A world that already knows more than your empire does is
a working lab whatever its designation, and seizing one makes every
future roll better. Losing a frontier world that out-grew your tier
stings twice.

One honesty note, because the game keeps the original's arithmetic:
the annual research chance is accumulated in a small integer that
can overflow. Up to a point more labs mean faster research; past
that point, adding another university can make the counter wrap and
the empire research *worse*. The twenty-lab cap exists in the game
as a mercy the DOS build could not offer.

## Independent worlds

Worlds do not sit still either, but they follow the capital rather
than the empire's books: any world *below* its capital's level has a
16% annual chance to climb one, and a world that has outrun the
capital regresses slowly -- technology out there is sustained by the
centre, not held permanently. Independent worlds have no capital and
advance on a bare 1-in-50 annual roll, which is still not nothing:
a backwater you leave alone for two hundred years may be
embarrassing to conquer by then. This is also why "wait and see" is not
free: the galaxy gets richer while you hold still.

## Trading technology

*Empire ▸ Trade technology* hands one of your technologies to
another empire -- free, no request in return, and no check whether
they already have it: two empires at the same level are each always
offering the other their entire tier. Gift technology to a small
neighbor to keep them ahead of a large one, or watch what the
pirates do with a jump drive. It is also the only way to give
someone something *after* you conquer their lab world -- a peace
offering the recipient can read as exactly what it is.

## What tech gates

Technology is checked in four places, all of them annoying at the
worst moment:

- **Designations** -- universities need jump tech to found, ninja
  worlds need starship, the gate-tier bases need gate tech.
- **Industry** -- you cannot run what you cannot name: bio-tech
  labs need level 7, shipyards at least level 4.
- **Population** -- some classes cannot support life at all below a
  given level; Gas Giants and Oceans wait for pre-warp.
- **Ships in space** -- a world's scouts only credit it with ships
  its own technology could have built, so a level-4 empire reporting
  a level-8 starship is being honest about having *seen* something,
  not about what it was.
