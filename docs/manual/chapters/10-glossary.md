# Glossary

> *The empire names a thing twice: once for the record, once for the
> crew that has to carry it.*
> -- Concordance of the Reconstruction, preface

Sector
:   One cell of the square map grid -- a star system and whatever
    sits in it: worlds, bases, gates, fleets, phenomena. The unit of
    all distances.

Chebyshev distance
:   The map's measure: the larger of the horizontal and vertical
    gaps between two sectors, so diagonal movement costs exactly the
    same as straight movement.

Capital
:   An empire's seat world. All your typed coordinates are measured
    from it, and it counts as a laboratory in every research roll.

Designation
:   The assigned job of a world -- agricultural, mine, shipyard base,
    university, outpost, and so on. Sets its principal industry and
    how its leftover industry is distributed.

Class
:   What a world physically is: Paradise, Ice world, Gas Giant,
    Ancient ruins. Sets trillum reserves, ground-defense bonus, and
    per-industry efficiency. Changed only by terraforming.

Scouted
:   The strongest grade of knowledge: you have seen the object and
    can see what it holds. Merely **known** objects are on the map
    but opaque; the detail panels and status tables demand scouting.

Base scan
:   The slow per-object coin flip a starbase runs each year, and the
    only way to discover something you had no idea existed.

Probe
:   A single-use sensor packet (*Fleet ▸ Probe*) sent to a known
    destination; reports what is there and returns by next year.

ISSP
:   Import/export self-sufficiency settings -- four sliders per
    world (chemicals, metal, supplies, trillum), 0 to 10, where 5
    means the world produces exactly what it consumes.

Supply link
:   The habit of food factories, mines and complexes of trading raw
    material across the eight neighbouring sectors. Starvation is
    usually a link problem before it is a fleet problem.

Trillium
:   The only fuel, and the rarest raw material. Ships, bases and
    jump drives burn it; worlds hold finite reserves set by their
    class. A fleet reporting *out of trillum* is a monument.

Fleet type
:   Standard, advanced-warp, penetrator, jump or hunter-killer
    force -- decided by what the fleet lacks, not what it has, and
    setting the fleet's sectors-per-year movement rate.

Jump
:   Movement through the gate network or a jump base's projection.
    Gate jumps cover any distance in one year. Jump traffic trips
    minefields; warp traffic does not.

Link (warp link)
:   A paired corridor structure, five years to build: a gate whose
    network is exactly one other end.

Disrupter
:   A structure that denies enemy jump movement in its neighbourhood
    while letting friendly warp fleets ride at ten sectors a year.

Group
:   Up to nine formations per attack: a set number of one ship type
    with its loaded troops. Groups manoeuvre and aim independently;
    this is the unit you command on the combat screen.

Shell
:   One of five altitudes -- deep space, high orbit, orbit,
    sub-orbit, ground. Combat resolves outermost first; anything not
    placed deliberately ends up parked in sub-orbit.

SRM field
:   Space-minefield; a site-built hazard that damages fleets making
    jump moves through its sector, and can be swept.

Command base
:   The mobile starbase. Scans, scouts, refuels, supports jump
    traffic, and shuffles one sector a year under orders.

Fortress
:   The base that multiplies sector defense (combat adjustment
    250%) and catapults departing fleets four sectors forward.

Industrial complex
:   A base that concentrates the surrounding sectors' raw material
    and passes its own overflow back to the mines.

GDM
:   Ground-defense missile. A world's fixed salvo, sized by
    technology, fired at anything in range of the surface.

LAM
:   Light attack missile. Precision first-strike stock carried by
    bases, fired at enemy fleets or a world's defenses; not
    interceptable, not recoverable.

Ninja
:   A legion that costs metal to produce, counts five-to-one against
    revolution and occupation duties, and wins fights they should
    lose.

Ambrosia
:   The product of bio-tech labs at 11.5 kilotons per billion
    people. Withholds: death and rage. Delivers: a 45% industry
    bonus from an addicted, satisfied population.

Revolution index
:   Per-world unrest, 0..100. Above 75, rebellion is possible;
    garrisons cap it, and quiet worlds under occupation resent the
    garrison.

Rebellion
:   The uprising that follows: either crushed by local troops, or
    successful, and the world becomes independent.

Auto-attack
:   The computer's attack routine -- the tactical AI that closes,
    aims and fires your fleets for you when you choose *auTo
    attack*, and that attacks you when it thinks the numbers are
    good.

Personality (NPE empire)
:   The computer empires' temperaments -- pirate, kingdom, guardian,
    berserker -- set by the scenario and reflected in what they
    build, where they raid, and how fast they escalate.

Ruins world
:   A relic of the old empire: a small research bonus, an easy
    conquest, and scenarios that disagree about what else.

Wanderer
:   An unidentified moving contact in a sensor report. The original
    reserved the name; you may go a hundred games without meeting
    one that exists.
